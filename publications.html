Job Market Paper

Job Market Paper
Mass Party-Switching From Below: Evidence from Panama
Manuscript in preparation

When do party members switch political parties? We know legislators switch parties strategically to advance their careers, but know far less about the strategic logics that govern mass party-switching. I propose a model in which a citizen’s hazard of changing formal party status is a function of their position in the electoral calendar, their organizational experience, and the competitiveness of their political environment. I test this model using the administrative registry of Panama’s nine major parties, and draw on focus groups with party members and organizers to interpret the strategic reasoning behind the patterns the registry reveals. Switching and withdrawal turn out to be governed by different logics: switching responds to organizational experience and the electoral calendar in ways withdrawal does not, and even the direction of switching — toward or away from the party in power — depends on how experienced a member is. Formal party membership operates as a strategic resource independent of the partisan identity a member expresses, a pattern with direct implications for how membership should be understood in any political system where comparable records exist.
